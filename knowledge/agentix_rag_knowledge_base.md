## 5. AI Voice Agents

### What the service is

AI Voice Agents are real-time conversational systems that can answer or place calls, understand spoken requests, use business knowledge, perform connected actions, and escalate to humans where appropriate.

Typical capabilities may include:
- inbound call reception;
- outbound calling;
- appointment booking;
- lead qualification;
- FAQ handling;
- customer support;
- follow-up calls;
- call routing;
- business-hours and after-hours coverage;
- CRM or spreadsheet updates;
- calendar integration;
- human handoff;
- conversation memory where required.

### Suitable problems

AI Voice Agents are relevant when a business:
- misses calls;
- receives repetitive phone inquiries;
- needs after-hours coverage;
- has slow lead response;
- manually qualifies many leads;
- spends staff time on routine appointment scheduling;
- needs scalable call handling;
- wants a voice interface connected to internal workflows.

### Example business outcomes

A voice agent can be designed to:
- answer common questions;
- understand why a caller is contacting the business;
- qualify the opportunity;
- check verified availability;
- book an appointment through connected systems;
- save lead details;
- route complex or sensitive cases to a human.

### Important limitations

A voice agent should not:
- invent availability;
- fabricate company policies;
- claim a booking occurred before confirmation;
- perform high-risk actions without appropriate controls;
- replace human judgment in workflows that require human approval.

Exact capabilities depend on the client's systems, integrations, call volume, policies, and required languages.

## 6. Multi-Agent Systems

### What the service is

Multi-Agent Systems coordinate multiple specialized AI agents or agent roles to complete larger workflows.

A multi-agent architecture is useful when one workflow contains distinct responsibilities, for example:
- research;
- qualification;
- planning;
- document analysis;
- tool execution;
- verification;
- reporting;
- human approval.

### Example use cases

- lead intake -> qualification -> follow-up -> CRM update;
- document intake -> extraction -> validation -> routing;
- support request -> knowledge retrieval -> action -> escalation;
- operations workflow with multiple systems and approval steps;
- research and analysis pipelines;
- business processes where separate agents handle specialized tasks.

### Design principle

Use multiple agents only when the workflow benefits from separation of responsibility. Do not use multi-agent architecture simply because it sounds more advanced.
